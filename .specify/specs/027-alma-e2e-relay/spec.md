## 1. Summary
The person can reach their own Alma from a phone without Tailscale, through a relay run by Alma, and the relay cannot read anything they do. It is a third transport for the remote channel of spec 026, chosen instead of Tailscale, never together with it: the PC makes an outbound connection to the relay, the phone connects to the relay, and between them every request and answer travels sealed by a session key that only the phone and the PC hold (an **end-to-end session**). The relay forwards ciphertext and learns only what any network middleman learns (that two endpoints talk, when, and how much). The security layer of 026 (device keys, scopes, step-up, limits, audit) is untouched: it does not know a relay exists. The relay is opt-in, off by default, turned on with its own native confirmation, and every scope still starts off. There are no Alma accounts. The relay is run by one person, the project maintainer, on voluntary donations. The phone app is the same PWA of 026 with no host written into it; it arrives from the relay, with a published hash and a best-effort gate that refuses a version the PC did not announce (it cannot stop an operator who deliberately serves a hostile app, which the notice says plainly).

## 2. Problem and goal
Spec 026 gives remote access only to people who install Tailscale on the PC and the phone, own a Tailscale account, enable HTTPS in its console and accept that the PC's name goes to public certificate logs. That is too much for many people. The alternative of a plain cloud relay was rejected in ADR 0021 because it adds a trusted third party with access to the house. The goal is a path with no extra account or app, where Alma's servers are not trusted with content because they never hold a key that opens it. Done means: a person with only Alma on the PC and a phone browser can choose the relay, pair a phone, and use whatever scopes they switch on, with the same approvals, step-ups, limits and audit as over Tailscale; a recording of everything the relay sees contains no message, note, picture, token or secret; a stranger who obtains the relay address gets nothing; a lost phone is cut off at once; and the 026 channel code was not changed to make any of it work.

## 3. Scope
**In:**
- A **relay transport** for the remote channel: a `RemoteTransport` that connects outward to the relay over a WebSocket (no listener opened on the PC) and feeds decrypted requests to the unchanged handler.
- The **end-to-end layer**: handshake, session keys, authenticated encryption of every request and answer, replay and tamper protection, its key binding into pairing. It lives inside the relay transport pair (PC side) and a matching client on the phone; the Tailscale path does not use it. WebCrypto only, no new dependency.
- The **relay server**: forwards opaque frames between a PC and its phones by an anonymous routing id, with limits and abuse resistance, without accounts and keeping nothing. Its code lives in this repository as a sixth architecture zone, `src/relay/`.
- Reuse of the 026 PWA: the end-to-end client is wired at its existing request seam (`pwaFetch`, `src/remote-client/http.ts`), the QR gains a relay form, the shell is served by the relay with a published hash and a PC-signed update gate (best-effort, BR-26).
- The transport selector (Tailscale or relay, one at a time), the relay's own switch and native confirmation, the tab and wizard step to choose and test the connection, the status and the closed list of failure causes.
- Docs in the same PR as the implementation: `docs/security/` (RISKS, CONTROLS, PRIVACY), ADR 0022, `docs/architecture.md` and the architecture test rules for the new zone, `docs/modules/remote-access.md`, the glossary (done for the terms already settled), the short privacy notice with an abuse contact, the written exit rule, and the amendments of §7.1.

**Out (and why):**
- Live camera video, WebRTC, a native phone app, Alma accounts, a self-hostable relay: out by inheritance (026 §3 Out, D9). The relay address is therefore a build-time constant, not a setting (BR-29).
- A paid tier: it would need accounts (BR-4). Donations are voluntary and no feature depends on one.
- Running Tailscale and the relay at the same time, and any automatic fallback from one to the other (BR-23).
- Any change to the 026 channel, `authorize()`, the route table or the Zod schemas of `src/shared/remote/api.ts`; any change to the Tailscale transport. If implementing this needs one, that is a defect of the design or a spec amendment, not a code change.
- Any new agent tool, any new `ToolRisk`, any route for the phone to read or change relay settings (026 BR-11).
- A message queue, a hold or store-and-forward of any kind at the relay (026 BR-33 is unchanged).
- Changing how Telegram and WhatsApp work, and the push path (026 BR-27: the PC sends the content-free push straight to the browser vendors; the relay does not carry it).
- Analytics or telemetry of any kind: the relay collects none about people or content (BR-16).
- Legal advice: the privacy notice and the exit rule are the maintainer's statements, not a legal opinion.

## 4. Users and stories
- **US-1** As a person without Tailscale, I want to reach my own Alma from my phone through Alma's relay, so that remote access does not need an extra account or app.
- **US-2** As a person, I want Alma's servers to be unable to read my messages, notes, pictures, approvals and tokens, so that I do not have to trust the operator with my house or my notes.
- **US-3** As a person, I want the relay off until I choose it, a system dialog an agent cannot click before it connects, and every scope off as before, so that nothing new reaches my PC by accident.
- **US-4** As a person, I want to pair a phone over the relay with the QR and the 6-digit code I compare, so that a relay that tries to sit in the middle is caught.
- **US-5** As a person, I want revoking a phone, the tray kill switch and turning remote access off to cut the relay path at once, so that a lost phone or a doubt is handled with one click.
- **US-6** As a person, I want the tab to tell me when the relay is not working and the concrete cause, so that I know what to do.
- **US-7** As the maintainer, I want the relay to be only a transport behind the one security layer, and a test that proves the channel did not change, so that adding it did not weaken 026.
- **US-8** As a person or reviewer, I want one written list of what the relay can see and what it keeps, who runs it and how to report abuse, so that the privacy claim can be checked.
- **US-9** As the operator, I want the relay to survive floods and misuse without accounts and without being able to look at content, and a written way out if I can no longer run it, so that it stays up while it exists and nobody is stranded when it ends.

## 6. Business rules
Each rule cites the decision it inherits or the human's answer (OQ-n of this spec, answered 2026-11-08).

- **BR-1** (026 BR-1, BR-2, ADR 0020) The relay is a third transport behind the one security layer. A transport only moves bytes and carries no authority. The channel has no `if` on the transport and does not mention the relay (AC-2.3).
- **BR-2** (026 BR-3, D3, owner's brief, OQ-3) The relay never sees content: it forwards ciphertext sealed by a session key it does not hold. The claim holds fully against a passive operator, a leaked relay, a network observer and a hosting account that only copies traffic. Against an operator who actively serves a hostile app shell it is narrowed by BR-26 but not closed; the residual (§7) is written in plain words in R-14 and ADR 0022.
- **BR-3** (026 BR-23, OQ-18 of 026) The end-to-end layer is the part of the relay transport pair that makes BR-2 true. The Tailscale path is not changed and does not use it (WireGuard and Serve's TLS stay its protection, ADR 0020).
- **BR-4** (026 BR-9) No Alma accounts. A device's identity is its key; the routing id is an address, not an identity of a person.
- **BR-5** (026 BR-10, BR-11, BR-29, AC-1.12, OQ-15) The relay is opt-in, off by default (and stays off by default until spec 001 delivers signing and Fuses, R-06). Choosing it has its own switch and its own native confirmation in `main`, separate from the enable dialog; the choice is kept across turn-ons of remote access and the enable dialog names it; turning remote access on still leaves every scope and sub-switch off. Narrowing (selecting Tailscale, turning remote access off, the tray kill switch, revoking) needs no confirmation.
- **BR-6** (026 BR-11) No agent tool touches the relay, its credentials or its status; the remote settings, relay ones included, are not changeable through `settings.patch`. This spec adds no tool and no `ToolRisk`.
- **BR-7** (026 BR-15) Secrets of the relay path (session keys, handshake secrets, relay credentials, the pairing secret and code) never appear in logs, audit entries, error messages, fixtures or prompts. What the PC must keep is sealed with `safeStorage` through `main/settings.ts`; session keys are never written.
- **BR-8** (026 BR-14, BR-1) The network position never authenticates, and neither does reaching the relay, knowing a routing id or holding an end-to-end session: every request still needs a valid device credential. The PC opens no listener for the relay; it connects outward.
- **BR-9** (026 BR-18, AC-3.8) The phone client is the 026 PWA with no host written into it. The relay is reached through its existing request seam.
- **BR-10** (026 BR-31) The limits of 026 are enforced by the channel on the decrypted request and are not lowered or raised by the relay. The relay's own limits (BR-28) are additional and apply to ciphertext.
- **BR-11** (026 BR-33, OQ-10) Remote works only while Alma runs and the PC is awake; there is no queue, and the relay buffers nothing: a frame for an offline PC is refused at once and frames in flight are held in memory only for the time forwarding takes. 026 BR-33 is unchanged.
- **BR-12** (026 BR-21 to BR-23, PD-1) Pairing, devices, sessions and revocation are the 026 flows. The end-to-end session sits beneath the bearer session and does not replace it (AC-3.10). Pairing over the relay uses the same QR, 3-minute secret, 6-digit code and native approval; the code covers the whole handshake transcript (BR-25). A stranger who obtains the address can lock pairing for 5 minutes and gain nothing; this is accepted and the relay adds no second pairing limit (OQ-7).
- **BR-13** (026 BR-6, BR-30, OQ-16) Audit is the 026 audit and its entries do not change shape. A relay outage is audited once, for the channel underneath, like the Tailscale one; a failed phone handshake is an audited denied failure, throttled like the other denied failures.
- **BR-14** (new, from BR-2; the list is an unavoidable fact of any relay, OQ-8) What the relay **never sees**: message and answer text, notes and their paths, images, camera stills, approvals, the route path and query, headers (including the bearer token), device ids and names, scope and sub-switch state, the pairing secret and code, step-up assertions, push subscriptions, session keys. What it **sees by construction**: the network address of each endpoint, TLS metadata, when a PC or phone connects and disconnects, whether the PC is online, the routing id (it is the host name of the per-PC address, so DNS and the TLS handshake of the phone's network show it too), and for each frame its direction, size and time. Sizes and timing are visible and can hint at what kind of thing a frame is (for example a large frame after a camera request); there is no padding, and the product says so plainly.
- **BR-15** (new, OQ-8) The relay **keeps nothing**: addresses, routing ids and counters live in memory for the life of a connection or a rate window and are gone after it; the relay's application logs hold no address, no id and no payload (AC-6.2). The hosting provider's own logs are minimized as far as the provider allows and what remains is disclosed in `PRIVACY.md`. The relay holds no routing-id binding across a restart or after the PC's connection ends (OQ-18, provisional): after a restart the first valid claimant wins, the end-to-end handshake still stops it from impersonating the PC (the phone checks the PC's key), and the harm is a denial of service until the real PC registers again. Known residual; if it ever bites, the mitigation path is one line: make the routing id commit to the PC's public key so the relay can check a claim without keeping anything.
- **BR-16** (new, from BR-2) The relay collects no telemetry about people or content and has no feature that makes an outbound request on a client's behalf (AC-5.9).
- **BR-17** (026 BR-27) Push stays content-free and goes from the PC straight to the browser vendors; the relay does not carry or originate pushes.
- **BR-18** (026 BR-16, OQ-13) The relay settings live in `main/settings.ts` with a type and default in `shared`; `shared` holds the relay frame schemas (Zod), the handshake state machine and the pure decisions (confirmations required, counters, framing, `acceptShell`); I/O is a thin shell (Constitution IV). The relay server's code is in this repository as a sixth architecture zone, `src/relay/`: it imports only itself and the frame contract in `src/shared/remote/`, nobody imports it, it touches no `electron`, `main`, `coordinator`, `renderer` or `remote-client`, it has its own build script and is excluded from the installer. `docs/architecture.md`, `tests/support/arch.ts` `RULES` and `tests/architecture.test.ts` cover it, each rule with a negative fixture (AC-5.11).
- **BR-19** (026 BR-19, D8) Failure texts name a concrete cause, never a generic error.
- **BR-20** (026 BR-17, BR-20) UI text is by dictionary key (§6.1): the PC's keys in the shared dictionaries, the phone's `remote.pwa.*` keys in the PWA's own es/en dictionary. UI work in the later stages loads the `console-ui` and `umbra-ui` skills first and the plan names the components and tokens it uses; none is invented here.
- **BR-21** (026 BR-2, Constitution V, owner's brief) Order and records: the relay is built after the 026 channel is stable (it is), ADR 0022 records the relay and the end-to-end layer, and `docs/security/` changes in the same PR as the code.
- **BR-22** (OQ-1) Operator and funding, as a fact: the relay is operated by the project maintainer, ftoledo, personally, who is its legal operator and pays for it. It runs on voluntary donations; there is no paid tier (it would need accounts, BR-4) and no feature depends on a donation. The operator's name and an abuse contact appear in the privacy notice (BR-31). Hard caps (BR-28) bound the bill and a written exit rule (BR-29) covers the day the maintainer can no longer run it. Which hosting platform runs the relay is a plan decision recorded in ADR 0022; the provider is disclosed in `PRIVACY.md`.
- **BR-23** (OQ-2) One transport at a time, chosen by a selector in the tab: Tailscale (the default) or Alma's relay. No composite transport, no both-at-once and no silent fallback from one to the other (BR-5). Each transport has its own paired devices: a device is reachable only over the transport it paired on, because its key and passkey live in that origin's storage; all of them count toward the 5.
- **BR-24** (OQ-4) Primitives: WebCrypto only, no new dependency: ephemeral ECDH on P-256 for key agreement, HKDF-SHA-256 for key derivation, AES-256-GCM for sealing with deterministic counter nonces, ES256 for the identity and device signatures (the primitive already used in `src/shared/remote/crypto.ts`). No weaker primitive is ever chosen at runtime (AC-3.7). Support for these on the phones Alma targets is to be verified in the plan, before implementation.
- **BR-25** (OQ-5) Handshake: ephemeral keys on both sides; the transcript is signed by the PC's identity key and, once the phone is paired, by the phone's device key; at pairing the phone verifies the PC's signature over the transcript against the fingerprint in the QR (the QR never passes through the relay), so a relay that terminates the handshake is caught before anything is sent; the QR secret and the 6-digit code stay exactly the 026 ones (the channel computes the code from inputs it generates, so mixing the secret into the key derivation or the transcript into the digits would change the channel; this refines the human's answer at plan stage, plan.md §1.2, and is cheap to revisit); per-direction counters inside the authenticated data reject replays and reordering; new session keys at every reconnect and at least every hour (aligned with the bearer session). On a tampered or replayed frame the frame is dropped and the end-to-end session ends (the safer default); the phone starts a new handshake on its next request. Plan-level details, not questions: the exact counter window, and how a response is tied to its request when several run in parallel (two held long polls plus short ones); the plan proposes them and the tests pin them.
  - *Built behaviours (found in verification, not asked by an AC):* a forged frame that carries a valid session id fails to open and ends that session, so the phone handshakes again (a denial-of-service lever for whoever can reach the id and learn a session id, not a confidentiality one; R-14 (m) to (r)); the PC keeps at most 32 unfinished handshakes and 256 live sessions, dropping the oldest past the cap.
- **BR-26** (OQ-3) Shell: the relay serves the PWA shell. Mitigations, all required and all best-effort: each release's shell hash is published with the release and shown in the PWA's Settings screen, so anyone can fetch the shell and compare; the PC announces the official shell hash of its own build inside the end-to-end session, signed by its identity key, and the app refuses a version the PC did not announce (`acceptShell`, AC-3.12); the bundle is the same one the 026 build produces, with no host written into it. The web platform lets the origin replace the service worker itself at every update check (about daily), so none of this stops an operator who deliberately serves a hostile shell or service worker: that operator can read that person's traffic at the first install, at any service-worker update and at any fresh load (a cleared site, or iOS evicting browser storage, 026 known limit). The gate catches a wrong, stale or partially tampered deployment and makes the lie comparable against the published hash; it is not a defense against that operator. This residual is stated in the wizard (`remote.relay.notice.shell`), in §7, in R-14 and in ADR 0022.
- **BR-27** (OQ-11) Connection style: the PC opens one outbound WebSocket to the relay; the phone sends plain HTTPS requests with a sealed body through the `pwaFetch` seam and has no WebSocket (consistent with 026 PD-6). The relay maps each phone request to a frame for the PC and returns the PC's answer as the HTTP response. Whether the PC's runtime WebSocket client avoids a `ws` dependency is a plan-level check; the relay server's platform decides its own server side.
- **BR-28** (OQ-7, OQ-12) Relay limits, additional to BR-10, defaults and tunable (changing one is a configuration change with its test updated, not a spec change; the tests read `REMOTE_RELAY_LIMITS`):

  | Limit | Default |
  |---|---|
  | One frame | 4 MiB (the largest legal answer, a 2 MB still as base64 in JSON, is about 2.7 MB) |
  | PC connections per routing id | 1 |
  | Phone connections per routing id | 15 (5 devices times 2 held long polls plus 1 short request) |
  | Frames per minute per routing id, both directions | 900 (above the channel's ceiling of 5 devices times 60 requests times 2 frames) |
  | Bytes per day per routing id | 1 GiB |
  | Connection attempts per address | 120 a minute (provisional default, tunable; twice the channel's 60 a minute per device so BR-10 holds). It counts every phone request and every PC connection attempt from the address: one request is one attempt |
  | Registration timeout (a PC socket that connects and never registers) | 10 seconds (provisional default, tunable; added during implementation, T31) |
  | Attempts at unknown ids per address | 10 a minute, then refused for 5 minutes |
  | Anonymous handshakes per routing id (the PC pays an ECDH and a signature for each) | 10 a minute |
  | Pause switch | an operator setting that makes every route answer `full` (the emergency stop when the budget ceiling is hit) |
  | At a cap | the new frame or connection is refused with `full`; an existing PC connection is never closed to make room; other ids are unaffected |
  | Global caps | the hosting platform's own included quota is the global cap, plus the pause switch; the platform has no hard spend limit to rely on, so the per-id quotas bound the bill (plan.md PD-7) |

  The routing id is 128 random bits; the PC registers it by signing a relay challenge with its identity key (OQ-7). The relay's per-day and per-minute counters live in memory (BR-15), so a restart resets them. The host platform's message-size and connection-time limits must be verified against its official documentation in the plan before these numbers are final.
  - *Built behaviours (found in verification, not asked by an AC):* the PC hands the channel only the `authorization`, `content-type` and `accept` headers of a phone's request (Host and Origin come from its own address, BR-33); a phone's own «forget this phone» call over the relay may lose its answer, because forgetting the device cuts the session that carried the call (the phone app treats forget as best effort).
- **BR-29** (OQ-17) Versioning, address and exit: (1) the handshake carries a protocol version; a build supports the current and the previous and refuses anything else with a concrete cause (AC-3.7); (2) the relay address is a build-time constant of the PC app with a development-only override honored only in an unpackaged app; it is not a setting, in line with "self-hostable relay is out" (AC-1.14); (3) a written exit rule covers the end of the relay, with a final app release that reports `remote.relay.fail.gone` (AC-8.7) and leaves Tailscale untouched. Its contents are PROVISIONAL (OQ-19, adopted while the human was away, to be confirmed on return, cheap to change): the relay ends when the monthly cost exceeds the budget ceiling for 3 consecutive months, or when the maintainer can no longer run it; 60 days of notice in the app, the docs and the release notes; at the end the hosting account and any provider logs are deleted. The budget ceiling is a named placeholder (`BUDGET_CEILING_PLACEHOLDER`) the maintainer replaces before the relay goes public; it blocks publishing (AC-6.7), never coding.
- **BR-30** (OQ-16) Status reuses `channels.state.*`. The closed list of relay causes is: unreachable (it includes the PC having no internet), refused, full, version, handshake (a phone's secure connection could not be verified) and gone (the relay has ended, BR-29). One desktop notification and one audit entry per outage; a wizard step to choose the connection; a relay test (AC-7.3). A new cause needs a spec change.
- **BR-31** (OQ-9) A short privacy notice with an abuse contact is published in the repository docs and shown in the wizard (`remote.relay.notice.operator`): who operates the relay, what it sees and keeps, how to report abuse. There are no separate terms of service. It is the maintainer's statement and not legal advice. The abuse contact and the jurisdiction are build-time constants with named placeholders (`ABUSE_CONTACT_PLACEHOLDER`, `JURISDICTION_PLACEHOLDER`); the facts are supplied by the maintainer (§8.2), and while a placeholder stands the publish check fails (AC-6.7). Coding and local runs are not blocked.
- **BR-32** (OQ-6) Address and origin: one origin per PC, `https://<routing id>.<relay domain>`, under a wildcard certificate (so per-PC names are not in public certificate logs). Device keys and passkeys are bound to that origin. A changed relay domain is handled like a PC rename (026 BR-38, BR-39, AC-4.8): the tab warns and offers to revoke the relay-paired phones so they can pair again. A phone paired over Tailscale pairs again for the relay and counts toward the 5.
- **BR-33** (OQ-14) Host and Origin: the relay transport stamps `Host` and `Origin` on the request it hands to the handler from its own relay-form address (AC-2.9). The channel and PD-1 are unchanged; the bearer token stays inside the sealed frame.

## 7. Constraints and risks
- **Layers touched** (`docs/architecture.md`): `shared` (relay frame schemas, the handshake state machine, counters and framing, `acceptShell`, settings type and default, `planRemoteChange` gains the relay confirmation, `REMOTE_RELAY_LIMITS`, i18n keys; all browser-safe, no `node:` imports, because the phone runs them); `coordinator/channels/remote` (a new relay transport implementing `RemoteTransport` and the end-to-end session; `channel.ts`, `authorize.ts` and `api.ts` unchanged; `transport.ts` gains one member of the `id` union); `main` (settings keys, the native confirmation, sealing of relay credentials); `renderer` (the tab and wizard gain the connection choice, the relay state and the notices); `remote-client` (the end-to-end client at the `http.ts` seam, the service-worker shell pin, the QR parse, the relay states, dictionary keys); `src/relay/` (the sixth zone, BR-18); docs. The transport interface returns a single `address` from `start()` and the channel derives its expected host and origin from it once; the relay transport returns the per-PC relay-form URL and stamps it on requests (BR-33), so the channel needs no change and only one transport runs (BR-23).
- **ToolRisk:** no new tool, so no new `ToolRisk` (BR-6).
- **Security / privacy:**
  - New third parties: the operator, the project maintainer personally (BR-22), and the relay's hosting provider (chosen in the plan, disclosed in `PRIVACY.md`). They see the metadata of BR-14 and keep what BR-15 allows. New data flow: ciphertext of everything the phone does, to those parties.
  - New attack surface: the PC's outbound WebSocket (no new inbound port); a per-PC address reachable from the whole internet, not only from a tailnet, so the unauthenticated pairing path is exposed to strangers who obtain the address, and the address is visible in DNS and in the TLS handshake of the phone's network (limits and lock-out: 026 BR-21, accepted in BR-12; the on-path visibility of the id is accepted and documented, provisional OQ-18); a known residual denial of service: after a relay restart the first valid claimant of a routing id wins until the real PC registers again, because the relay keeps no binding (BR-15; one-line mitigation path: the id commits to the PC's public key); an end-to-end handshake written in the project, whose parsing and nonce handling are security-critical (adversarial tests and a recorded review are part of the plan); a relay server that is a denial-of-service target (BR-28).
  - **Honest limit of the claim (BR-2, BR-26).** A web app cannot prove its own code. The relay serves the PWA shell, so an operator who deliberately serves a hostile shell to one person can read that person's traffic. End-to-end encryption protects against a passive operator, a leaked relay or its database, a network observer and a compromised hosting account that only copies traffic; it does not protect against an operator who actively alters the shell. The mitigations do not close it and the plan found that one is weaker than first written: the origin can replace the service worker itself at every update check, so the PC-signed gate (`acceptShell`) cannot stop a deliberate swap; what remains is the published hash (anyone can fetch the shell from the relay and compare, which makes a targeted lie harder to hide but not impossible, since an operator can serve different files to different people). The first install, every service-worker update and any fresh load after a cleared site or after iOS evicts browser storage (026 known limit) run whatever the relay serves at that moment. The stricter alternative, shell served only by the PC over Tailscale once and the relay used afterwards from that same origin (OQ-3 option c), stays available if the human decides this residual is too large. Here the operator is the maintainer personally and the code is open, which is a mitigation by trust, not a technical one. This is the same kind of residual as 026 PD-7 (the fingerprint defends against a wrong host, not a malicious PC). ADR 0022 and R-14 say it in plain words, and ADR 0021's reason for not choosing an Alma relay ("a trusted third party with access to the house") is answered only to this extent.
  - Traffic analysis: sizes, timing and online state of the PC are visible to the relay by construction (BR-14); no padding.
  - New stored personal data: relay credentials on the PC, sealed with `safeStorage`. The relay keeps nothing by design (BR-15); the hosting provider's logs are outside Alma's control beyond minimizing them. Nothing new on the phone beyond its device key (per origin, BR-32) and the app shell.
  - Docs in the same PR (Constitution V): `docs/security/RISKS.md` (R-14 amended, plus a new row for the relay operator if the human wants it separate), `CONTROLS.md` (a relay row and an end-to-end layer row, plus an amendment of the "only the owner uses the remote channel" row), `PRIVACY.md` (the provider rows, retention, traffic-analysis limit; the Tailscale sentence of line 60 stays true only while Tailscale is selected).
- **Performance / platform:** Windows-first for the PC; the relay adds a hop and a crypto step per request, inside the 30-second timeout and the 25-second long poll (AC-5.8). Plan-level checks before implementation, each against official documentation: browser support of the BR-24 primitives on the phones Alma targets; the hosting platform's message-size, connection-time and wildcard-certificate support (BR-28, BR-32); whether the PC runtime's WebSocket client avoids a `ws` dependency (BR-27).
- **Dependencies, not scope:** 026 and 028 implemented (they are); R-01 closed; R-06 and spec 001 for the default (BR-5); the hosting platform decision (plan, ADR 0022) before any task that needs a server.
- **ADR:** [ADR 0022](../../../docs/adr/0022-alma-e2e-relay.md) records the relay and the end-to-end layer, the hosting platform, and the decisions of OQ-1 to OQ-19 (OQ-18 and OQ-19 provisional); it answers ADR 0021's "Cloud relay operated by Alma: not chosen" with the honest limit above, and carries the exit rule. ADR 0020's stale body text ("Status is `proposed` ... nothing is in the code yet") was fixed with the plan.
