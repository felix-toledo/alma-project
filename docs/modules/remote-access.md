# Remote access

> Control Alma from a phone browser: chat, approvals, house, cameras, notes and conversations over Tailscale (or, opt-in and not yet deployed, Alma's end-to-end relay), paired by QR, everything off by default. Spec: [026 · Remote access](../../.specify/specs/026-remote-access/spec.md), extended by [028 · Remote notes, conversations and phone app polish](../../.specify/specs/028-remote-vault-and-threads/spec.md) and [027 · Alma's end-to-end relay](../../.specify/specs/027-alma-e2e-relay/spec.md); [031 · Attachments from the phone's chat](../../.specify/specs/031-remote-chat-attachments/spec.md) lets the phone attach pictures and files. Test status: all 11 features with automated tests (210+ assertions across the `remote-*` suites); the phone-side checks (Tailscale on iOS/Android, install prompt, push delivery, passkey on a real phone) are manual (`S1`–`S3`).

## What it does

- Serves a phone web app (PWA) from the person's own PC over their Tailscale tailnet: the PC exposes nothing to the public internet.
- Can instead connect through Alma's relay (spec 027, opt-in, off by default, one transport at a time): the PC dials out, the phone talks to a per-PC address, and everything travels sealed end to end so the relay forwards ciphertext and keeps nothing. See «The relay» below.
- Pairs one phone at a time with a QR plus a 6-digit code typed on the PC; the phone also registers a passkey so sensitive actions ask for the fingerprint or face again.
- Gives the phone six scopes, each a separate on/off switch on the PC: chat, answer approvals, house control, camera pictures, **Notes** (read the active vault, read-only) and **History** (read other conversations), plus the sub-switches locks and alarms (under house), **continue conversations** (under History) and, since spec 031, **Send files from the phone** (under Chat). Everything starts off.
- Lets the phone read notes and conversations without keeping anything: the phone stores no note and no conversation, and the vault is never written from the phone.
- Reuses the same tools the desktop uses for chat, approvals, house and cameras, so the same rules, confirmations and audit apply (PD-10).
- Notifies the phone when an approval is waiting: the push carries no content, the phone fetches the question when opened (PD-15).
- Since spec 033, with History on, notifies the phone when the morning greeting is delivered (a fixed text, no content) and opens the greeting conversation once at the next opening.

## How to use it

**Turn it on.** «Configuración» → Acceso remoto → «Activar el acceso remoto». The setup wizard walks through it: install Tailscale, sign in with the same account on PC and phone, serve over HTTPS, test the connection, then pair the phone. Each step explains what to do and what can go wrong; the wizard remembers where it stopped.

**Pair a phone.** On the last wizard step (or the pairing card later), the PC shows a QR and a fingerprint (two words). Scan the QR with the phone camera, open the link, type the 6-digit code shown on the PC, and confirm the fingerprint words match. The PC asks for confirmation in a native dialog; only then the phone is paired. At most 5 phones, each also registers a passkey during pairing (PD-5).

**Use the phone.** The PWA has five destinations, always in this order: Chat, Notes, «Casa» (house), «Cámaras» (cameras) and «Ajustes» (settings); approvals show on the Chat screen. See "Phone app navigation" below. Chat is text of up to 8000 characters per message, plus pictures and files when the PC's «Send files from the phone» sub-switch is on (see «Attachments from the phone» below). Approvals show the full request with **Permitir** (allow once) and **Rechazar** (reject) — like voice, the phone never answers «Siempre permitir»; a question already answered elsewhere says so. «Casa» lists the same devices as the desktop with the same confirmations; opening a lock or disarming the alarm asks for the passkey. «Cámaras» shows one still per camera («Ver foto»). The phone can be forgotten from its own «Ajustes» tab («Olvidar este teléfono») or revoked from the PC at any moment.

**Revoke.** «Configuración» → Acceso remoto → the device card → «Revocar». The phone stops working immediately; an idle phone (15 days without use) and an expired approval question are swept automatically. Turning the whole feature off stops the server at once (the tray icon stays only if «run in tray» is also on).

## How it works

Pieces:

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `src/coordinator/channels/remote/` | The channel: HTTP API, pairing, sessions, one flow per scope (`chat.ts`, `approvals.ts`, `home.ts`, `cameras.ts`, `notes.ts`, `threads.ts`), `runs.ts` (run ties), `thread-view.ts` (message windows), `uploads.ts` (spec 031: the pending uploads of the phone; the store itself is `src/coordinator/attachments/phone.ts`), `quiet.ts` (keeps channels silent about phone-started runs) |
| `src/shared/remote/`, `src/shared/vault/`, `src/shared/threads.ts` | Pure core: API contract (`api.ts`, `wire.ts`), limits (`limits.ts`), authorization (`authorize.ts`), crypto (`crypto.ts`), pairing and step-up machines, WebAuthn, audit/activity row builders, `status.ts`; vault path rules (reachable, excluded, listing, find by name, asset resolution) and the one definition of which conversations are listable |
| `src/remote-client/` | The PWA: screens, offline shell, keys in IndexedDB; `lib/` holds the pure rules (navigation, screen states, refusal-to-text mapping, markdown links) |
| `src/renderer/src/remote/` | PC tab: settings hook, wizard, pairing/devices/scopes/exclusion/activity cards |
| `src/main/` | Tray state, host confirmations, sealed storage of the channel keys |

**One security layer, transport as strategy** (BR-1). `RemoteChannel` owns every check; the transport (Tailscale Serve, or the in-memory test transport) only moves bytes. The network listener binds loopback only and refuses anything that is not from the tailnet (T0, PD-8).

**Pairing** (PD-7). The PC creates an offer (QR payload, single use, 3 minutes) and a 6-digit code derived from it (HMAC-SHA256, 3 attempts, then a 5-minute lockout; 5 offers per minute). The phone proves possession of the offer, the person confirms on the PC, and the passkey registration (challenge bound to offer + PC nonce) completes the binding. The fingerprint (two words) defends against pairing with the wrong host on the tailnet.

**Sessions.** The phone holds an opaque Bearer token (1 hour, in memory on the PC, PD-1); the refresh token (sealed, 15 days of idle) renews it silently. Every request re-checks: known device, scope on, sensitive action needs a fresh step-up, camera pictures need the cameras scope.

**Step-up** (BR-24). Locks, alarms and gate/garage covers need a passkey assertion made in the last 60 seconds for that exact device + thing + action (single use, fail closed). A stolen unlocked phone can chat and answer approvals until revoked, but cannot open or disarm anything without the owner's biometrics.

**Narrow API.** A closed list of routes (`ROUTES` in `src/shared/remote/api.ts`; a snapshot test fails when one is added without a scope, an audit class and a strict body schema), each validating its body at the edge: `chat/send|new|stop`, `state`, `approvals`, `approvals/answer`, `home/things|control`, `cameras`, `cameras/still`, `status`, plus `hello`, `pair/*`, `session/*`, `stepup/challenge`, `push/*`, `pong`, `forget`. Spec 028 adds eight POST routes, all behind a session, the pure authorization verdict and the shared rate limit:

| Route | Scope | Audited |
|---|---|---|
| `notes/list` (a folder), `notes/find` (by name) | Notes | no |
| `notes/read` (a note), `notes/image` (an image a note embeds or links) | Notes | yes: vault-relative path, outcome |
| `threads/list` (30 a page) | History | no |
| `threads/read` (last 30 text messages, or an earlier page; with `since` + `wait` a long poll) | History | yes: conversation id, outcome (the `since` polling is not) |
| `threads/send` | History + continue conversations | yes: conversation id, outcome |
| `threads/stop` | History | yes: conversation id, outcome |

`GET /api/status` returns exactly the on/off state of each scope and sub-switch (no device id, exclusions or camera ids). No route writes, renames, deletes or searches the vault, and none renames or deletes a conversation or changes its agent, model or folder. Chat input is text (8000 chars) and, since spec 031, an optional list of pending upload ids; the model answer is cut at 1500 chars for approvals; polls are long (25 s) so the phone makes no WebSocket (PD-6).

**Attachments from the phone** (031). Three more POST routes, all behind a session, the pure authorization verdict and the shared 60-a-minute limit, plus an optional strict `attachments` id list on the two sends. The Files sub-switch (`remote.sendFiles`) gates them.

| Route | Needs | Audited |
|---|---|---|
| `chat/attach` (one file into the Chat tab's shared thread) | Chat + Send files | yes: device, outcome, kind and size when stored (`remote.attachments.put`) |
| `threads/attach` (one file into a conversation by id) | History + continue conversations + Send files | yes, same entry |
| `attachments/remove` (the chip's X; the phone's own pending upload only) | Chat or History, and Send files | yes, as a change (`remote.attachments.remove`), no id |
| `chat/send`, `threads/send` with `attachments: [ids]` | their usual gate; Send files too, only when the body names ids | as today, with the number of attachments in the detail |

- **One file is one request**, at most 700 000 bytes decoded (`PHONE_ATTACH.maxFileBytes`, its own constant; `REMOTE_LIMITS` did not change). The body is `{name, size, base64}`, strict, with the claimed `size` equal to the decoded byte count. The answer is the stored `{id, name, kind, size}`. A big picture is shrunk on the phone first (a canvas re-encode as `imagen-<timestamp>.jpg`, up to five steps, one file at a time); that is a convenience, the PC re-checks everything.
- **What the PC does with a put** (`UploadsFlow`, then `PhoneFiles` in `src/coordinator/attachments/phone.ts`, which uses spec 030's `classifyFile`, `isSecretLikeName` and `AttachmentStore`): the target must exist and not be a company's (`gone`, the same answer for all three); a run going on the thread answers `busy`; more than 5 pending for the device is `too-many`; over 700 000 bytes `too-large`; a secret-looking name `secret-name` (no override); type and content by `classifyFile` (`file-type`, `file-content`, `file-empty`, `file-malformed`); a provider that would read it and has not been shown the notice `notice-needed`; a failed write `storage`. Nothing is stored on a refusal, and nothing is sent to a model provider by a put.
- **The pending list** lives in memory (`PendingUpload`: id, device, thread, put time). A file is pending until a send binds it. It is deleted at once on removal (own uploads only; any other id deletes nothing and answers the same), after 15 minutes, when the device is revoked, forgotten or idle-expired (`onDeviceGone`), when remote access is turned off or the kill switch is used (`stop()`), when Send files or Chat is turned off (`reconcile`), and for a Chat-tab upload when «New conversation» replaces the shared thread. It is **not** purged when only History or continue conversations is turned off (they expire in 15 minutes). After a restart nothing is pending and the files wait for spec 030's 30-day sweep. A file that cannot be deleted when its upload stops being pending is left to that sweep and recorded as one `remote.attachments.discard` entry (outcome error, device-less, no id and no name).
- **A send** claims the ids (`claimUploads`): 1 to 5, this device's, for this conversation, unexpired and distinct. Any other case refuses the whole send as `unavailable` with one answer and leaves the other uploads pending; the provider notice is checked again (`notice-needed`). The ids are bound after the user message is committed, through the same run entry the PC uses (`composeUserMessage`), and from then on the files follow spec 030. A refused send leaves them pending until they expire; the phone keeps its text and chips.
- **Projection.** Both thread views carry, for a message with attachments, a list of `{name, kind, size}` and nothing else (`phoneAttachmentViews`); an attachment-only message is listed. The id, path, bytes and a thumbnail never leave the PC, and the phone shows the names as plain text.
- **`lastOpenedAt` on the phone.** The first read of a conversation by id (no `before`, no `since`) calls `markOpened`; held repeats, «load earlier», the list and the Chat tab's state poll do not. A put also restarts the target conversation's 30 days (the store's `opened` hook), even if the file is never sent.
- **The sub-switch.** `remote.sendFiles` is off at every turn-on, turned on through a native confirmation (`main.remote.attachments`) that names the providers and records them as acknowledged on Allow, cannot outlive Chat, and `GET /api/status` returns it as `sendFiles` only while Chat is on. The key is optional on the wire: a phone talking to an older PC reads a missing key as off. On the PC it is a checkbox under Chat in the «Remote access» tab (`ScopesCard`).
- **Tools.** No new tool and no new `ToolRisk`: `read_attachment` is `read` and never asks, so it works in phone-started runs like any other; every other tool of the run keeps its risk and approvals.

**Notes** (028). Reads go through the active vault at each request. The pure `isReachable` rejects dot segments, `node_modules`, `..`, absolute paths and excluded folders, and the file must be listed by the vault's own tree; bytes come from `KnowledgeBase.readBytes`, confined like the desktop. Every unreachable path (missing, hidden, excluded, outside the vault, a junction out) gets the same `unavailable` answer. Listing a hidden, excluded or missing folder returns an empty folder, so existence is never revealed. `.md` notes are served as UTF-8 text (over 1 MB: refused, never cut); images up to 2 MB as base64; any other type is `unsupported` but still listed. A path in a different casing is served under the vault's real casing. The name filter searches file paths (not content) of the whole reachable vault, up to 50, with the same fuzzy rule as the PC's quick switcher. With no active vault, or its folder gone, the phone gets `vault: none`.

**Conversations** (028). The list is what the PC's Chat tab lists (`listableThreads`; company-agent threads never), newest first; the shared remote thread carries a `current` mark. A missing id and a company id answer the same `gone`. Sending runs the conversation's own agent through the one run entry the PC uses; the user message is committed first. A device has at most one phone-started run (`run-active`), a conversation already running anywhere answers `busy` (nothing queued, nothing interrupted), and only the starter can stop its run. The conversation the phone writes into follows live (long poll); others are snapshots with a refresh control. Sending into the shared remote thread's id behaves like the Chat tab and never re-points it. The run follows the app's normal approval policy (see Security and privacy).

**Run ties and approvals** (028). `RunTies` (memory only) records which device started which run. A device sees and answers the approvals of the shared remote thread (as in 026) and of its own by-id runs, only with the approve scope on; with approve off the phone shows "waiting for an approval on the PC" and the approval stays pending for the other surfaces. The content-free push for a by-id run goes to its starter only. When access is cut, the runs are aborted through the PC's own abort (their approvals resolve as denied): the kill switch, remote off, and a revoked, forgotten or swept device abort that device's runs; History or the sub-switch off aborts the by-id runs. A run in the shared thread survives turning only the chat scope off. `quiet.ts` sits between the coordinator and Telegram, WhatsApp and the voice assistant: while a phone-started run is going, its approval prompt and streamed text are not forwarded to those channels, and an answer from them for a phone-owned approval is ignored (the PC window still gets every event and can answer).

**Push** (PD-15). The PC signs its own VAPID key and sends empty pushes through the browser vendor's service when an approval lands on a remote thread and the approve scope is on. The Service Worker wakes, shows a generic «Tenés una aprobación pendiente» ("You have a pending approval") with no details, and the phone fetches the question over Tailscale when opened.

**The morning greeting** (spec 033, amending 026 BR-27 and PD-15 for this one push). When the nightly dream's greeting is delivered (see [vault and knowledge](vault-and-knowledge.md#the-nightly-dream-spec-033)), `notifyGreeting` (`channels/remote/push.ts`) sends, only with remote access on and the **History** scope on, one push to every subscribed device. Unlike the approval push it has a body: the one-character fixed code `g` (`GREETING_PUSH_CODE`), encrypted end to end to the subscription's `p256dh`/`auth` with RFC 8291 `aes128gcm` (`encryptPushPayload`, `shared/remote/push.ts`, WebCrypto only), with `Content-Encoding: aes128gcm`, `Content-Type: application/octet-stream` and `TTL: 3600`. The push service sees an encrypted body of fixed size, never text. The Service Worker shows «Alma te dejó el saludo del día» (`remote.pwa.push.greeting`) when the decrypted data is `g`, the approval text otherwise (shell version v11). A failure is silent and not retried; the greeting conversation exists anyway.

**The greeting landing** (spec 033 BR-29). At delivery the dream service records a pending landing for every paired device. The device's first `GET /api/status` after that consumes it, whatever the scopes; only with History on does the answer carry `landing` (the conversation id), and the phone app then opens that conversation's screen once. With History off that first opening is the landing and the app opens as today. Kept across PC restarts in `dream-state.json`; erased with «Borrar mi historial».

**Audit.** Remote changes and camera pictures are audited like desktop ones (`remote.authorize`, `remote.run`, `remote.pair.approved`, `remote.revoke`, `remote.stepup.*`), plus the four failures (wrong Tailscale identity, unknown/expired device, scope off, bad step-up). The PC activity card reads them back newest-first, at most 50 rows.

## The relay (spec 027)

> Status: **built, not deployed.** The code, its tests and the docs exist; no relay runs on Cloudflare yet, and a packaged app offers the option only while the relay domain, the abuse contact, the jurisdiction and the budget ceiling are real values (`relayAvailable`; they are, since the maintainer supplied them, see «Deploying»); a development run is never held back. Spec: [027 · Alma's end-to-end relay](../../.specify/specs/027-alma-e2e-relay/spec.md); decision record: [ADR 0022](../adr/0022-alma-e2e-relay.md).

**What it is.** A second way to reach the PC for people who do not want Tailscale on the phone. In the Remote access tab, a selector chooses the transport: **Tailscale** (the default, unchanged) or **Alma's relay**. Only one runs at a time; there is no fallback from one to the other. Choosing the relay asks for a native confirmation that says what the relay sees. Each transport has its own paired phones (a phone is reachable only over the one it paired on); all of them count toward the 5.

**How it connects.** The PC opens one outbound WebSocket to `wss://<routing id>.<relay domain>/relay/v1/pc` and exposes no inbound port. The phone sends plain HTTPS requests to `https://<routing id>.<relay domain>` through the same `pwaFetch` seam as before: `GET /relay/v1/info` tells the phone app it is on a relay, and each request is one sealed frame `POST`ed to `/relay/v1/frame` with one sealed frame back. The routing id is 26 random lower-case base32 characters, made on first use and kept sealed with the OS store (`remote/relay-sealed`); it is the address and the only identity the relay knows. The channel code (`channel.ts`, `authorize.ts`, `api.ts` and the flows) is the 026 code, unedited: the relay transport hands it requests stamped with its own Host and Origin.

**End-to-end layer.** Beneath the bearer session, the PC and the phone run a handshake (ephemeral ECDH P-256, HKDF-SHA-256, AES-256-GCM with counter nonces, ES256 signatures, all WebCrypto) so the relay carries only ciphertext it cannot open. The PC signs the handshake with its identity key and the phone checks it against the fingerprint in the QR (which never passes through the relay) or the key it stored; a paired phone signs with its device key. Counters reject replays; a tampered frame ends the session; sessions last an hour and the phone renews at 55 minutes by its own clock. The 6-digit pairing code and the 3-minute secret are exactly the 026 ones.

**Shell gate.** The relay serves the phone app and `shell-manifest.json` (version, a SHA-256 per file and a root hash, written by `pnpm run build:remote`). The PC announces the root hash of its own official build inside the secure session, signed with its identity key, and the phone (`acceptShell`) refuses an app the PC did not announce and shows the running hash in Settings. Limit, stated everywhere: this catches a stale, wrong or partly altered deployment; it does not stop an operator who deliberately replaces the service worker ([R-14](../security/RISKS.md) (n)).

**Status and failures.** The tab shows the usual `channels.state.*` with one concrete cause from a closed list: unreachable, refused, full, version, handshake, gone. One desktop notification and one audit entry per outage; the PC retries by itself with backoff, except for version and gone. A relay test in the wizard checks, in order, the PC's internet, that the relay answers, that it accepts this PC's registration and, if a phone is paired over it, that the phone answers a test message. If the relay's address changes (a rename-like event) the tab warns and offers to revoke the relay-paired phones so they pair again.

**Limits** (`REMOTE_RELAY_LIMITS` in `src/shared/remote/relay-limits.ts`; tunable defaults, the tests read them):

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Frame size | 4 MiB |
| PC connections per routing id | 1 (a second one with the same key replaces the first; another key is refused) |
| Phone requests in flight per id | 15 |
| Frames per minute per id | 900 |
| Bytes per day per id | 1 GiB |
| Connection attempts per minute per address (any id) | 120 |
| Attempts at ids with no live PC per minute per address | 10, then 5 minutes blocked |
| Anonymous handshakes per minute per id | 10 |
| Request timeout at the relay | 35 seconds |
| Time for a connected socket to prove its key | 10 seconds |

The counters live in memory (the per-address ones in one shared object, so probing many ids from one address adds up) and a restart resets them. `RELAY_PAUSED=1` is the emergency stop: every phone request answers «full» without waking a room and every PC socket is turned away.

**Where the code is.** `src/relay/` is a separate architecture zone: platform-free logic (`room.ts`, `registration.ts`, `limits-state.ts`, `route.ts`, `body.ts`, `gate.ts`, `answer.ts`, `counters.ts`) tested in Vitest against `tests/support/relay-fake.ts`, and a thin Cloudflare adapter (`src/relay/cloudflare/worker.ts`, `room-object.ts`, `gate-object.ts`) with hand-written platform types (`src/relay/platform.d.ts`). On the PC side: `relay-transport.ts`, `relay-connection.ts`, `relay-host.ts`, `relay-store.ts`, `relay-controller.ts` under `src/coordinator/channels/remote/`. On the phone: `src/remote-client/relay/`. The pure parts (frames, key schedule, shell gate) are `src/shared/remote/relay-*.ts`. The relay is not in the installer: `pnpm run build` never touches it and `pnpm run build:relay` only typechecks it.

**What the operator can and cannot see** is in [PRIVACY.md](../security/PRIVACY.md) (written plainly, with what is still to be verified about Cloudflare's own logs).

**Deploying (the maintainer, never a script).** The facts are supplied and set in `src/shared/remote/relay-config.ts`: `RELAY_DOMAIN` `almaproject.app`, `ABUSE_CONTACT` `abuse@almaproject.app` (a Cloudflare Email Routing address the maintainer forwards; no personal address is in the repository), `JURISDICTION` `Argentina`, `BUDGET_CEILING` `USD 5 per month`; `relay/wrangler.toml` carries the same domain in its route and zone, and a test keeps the two equal. In order: put the domain on the maintainer's Cloudflare account with its nameservers (the **Full setup**, not a CNAME setup), because Workers Routes only work on a zone Cloudflare serves; create a **proxied wildcard DNS record** in that zone, name `*`, type `AAAA`, content `100::` (proxied), so that `<routing id>.almaproject.app` reaches the Worker, since a Workers Route does not create DNS records (the bare domain needs no record for the relay). **The domain also serves the project's landing page on Vercel and that must keep working:** before changing the nameservers, note every existing DNS record (the landing's `A`/`CNAME` and any `TXT` verification), check that Cloudflare imported them, and set the landing's records to **DNS only** (grey cloud), never proxied, so that traffic for the landing never reaches the relay's Worker (the route `*.almaproject.app/*` only catches proxied hosts, and answers any host that is not a routing id with 404); explicit records such as `www` take precedence over the wildcard. Do not remove the Vercel project or its domain settings. If the domain's nameservers are currently Vercel's, recreate the landing's records in Cloudflare first. After the switch, open the landing to confirm it still loads. Then run `pnpm run relay:publish-check` (it must exit 0; it fails, naming each placeholder, if one is ever put back); run `pnpm run build:remote` so the phone app and `shell-manifest.json` exist in `out/remote`; sign in with `wrangler login` (preferred over creating an API token: nothing long-lived to store or leak) and deploy with a pinned wrangler that is not installed in this repository: `pnpm dlx wrangler@<pinned version> deploy --config relay/wrangler.toml` (the person who deploys chooses the version and records it). Then verify on the real platform what the offline checks could not: spikes V2 (message and body limits, memory with 15 requests of 4 MiB, the free plan's allowance, whether a spend cap exists, host-name case and the wildcard certificate), V3 (a request held for 25 seconds while the object may sleep), V8 (the issued certificate), V9 (what Cloudflare logs), and the real-phone walk. Set `RELAY_PAUSED` to `1` once to prove the stop works.

**Exit rule (provisional until the maintainer confirms it, task H4).** The relay ends when its monthly cost exceeds the budget ceiling (`BUDGET_CEILING`, USD 5 per month) for 3 consecutive months, or when the maintainer can no longer run it. There are 60 days of notice in the app, the docs and the release notes; a final release reports `remote.relay.fail.gone` and points to Tailscale, which is untouched; at the end the hosting account and any provider logs are deleted. Abuse contact: `abuse@almaproject.app`; jurisdiction: Argentina. If any of the three notice constants were ever a placeholder again, publishing would be blocked (`pnpm run relay:publish-check`), never coding.

## Phone app navigation

- **Bar.** A persistent bar with five destinations in a fixed order (`NAV_ORDER`): Chat, Notes, «Casa», «Cámaras», «Ajustes». The current one is marked and exposed to assistive technology (`aria-current="page"`). A destination whose scope is off stays visible and shows its state ("turned off on the PC"), because scopes can change while the app is open.
- **Chat.** The shared remote thread as in 026 (send, stop, "New conversation", approvals). Its header has a Conversations entry: the list (30 a page, "load more", the shared thread marked as this phone's chat) and, from it, a conversation. Reading shows the last 30 messages with "load earlier". The compose box appears only when continue conversations is on; the conversation this phone writes into follows live, the others show a snapshot with a refresh control. Approval cards of the phone's own by-id run are marked with their conversation.
- **Attaching (031).** The compose box (Chat and a conversation) has an attach button (`Paperclip`, 44 px) that opens the phone's own chooser (camera, library and files in one sheet) for several files; its `accept` list is a convenience and names the picture types (`image/jpeg`, `png`, `webp`, `gif`) instead of `image/*` so iOS converts a HEIC photo to JPEG. Each file becomes a chip (name, state: preparing, uploading, ready, failed; an X that aborts or removes it); chips upload one at a time and the send button stays off while one is preparing or uploading. With the PC's switch off the button is shown disabled with the reason. Every refusal is one concrete text naming the file; «retry» is offered only for the PC not answering or a failed upload. Sent messages show their attachments under the text as a kind icon, the name and the size (plain text, no thumbnail, no link). Nothing is queued, stored or replayed: chips and bytes live in the page's memory.
- **Notes.** Folder list (folders first), a name field that searches the whole reachable vault, and the note reader (GitHub-flavored Markdown without raw HTML, frontmatter hidden, `[[wiki links]]` as plain text, relative links open inside the reader under the same rules, external links open in the browser only on a tap, remote images never fetched, images lazy-loaded so a note with many images does not trip the rate limit).
- **Back.** The system back gesture and the on-screen control go up one level (note to its folder, folder to its parent, conversation to the list, the list to Chat) and leave the app only from a top level. Position is kept while the app stays open and is never stored.
- **States.** Every screen that loads from the PC has the same set: loading (after a short threshold), empty (what is missing), error (one concrete cause), scope off, PC unreachable (retries by itself with backoff; a typed message stays in the box) and revoked. "Retry" is offered only where trying again can help, not for a scope that is off, a revoked phone, a note that is too big or a rate limit. A pure mapping gives every refusal exactly one text, and a test fails when a new refusal has none. On any non-content state the content is dropped from memory; coming back to a screen asks the PC again.
- **Settings.** The connection state, a read-only list of what this phone can do now (chat, approve, house, cameras, locks and alarms, notes, conversations, continue conversations, send files, each on or off), the app version and «Olvidar este teléfono» with its confirmation. No language switch (the app follows the phone) and no scope controls (those are PC-only).
- **Look.** Dark only, `umbra-ui` adapted to touch, the slow background kept, reduced motion honored, without sounds or music. Texts are keys of the PWA's own Spanish and English dictionaries, checked for parity.

## Security and privacy

- Off by default at every level: feature, scopes, Serve. Turning the feature on re-asks for every scope; turning it off stops the server immediately.
- The threat model is explicit about what pairing does and does not defend ([RISKS.md](../security/RISKS.md) R-14): the fingerprint defeats pairing with the wrong host, not a compromised PC; a stolen token is valid up to 1 hour; an unlocked phone chats and approves until revoked.
- Secrets (channel keys, push keys, refresh tokens) are sealed with the OS keychain, never logged, never in fixtures or prompts; the window only learns whether they exist.
- Push carries no content through the vendor; camera stills travel only over the tailnet; the tailnet identity of every request is checked (PD-8).
- Privacy: pairing metadata (names, dates) is exported without key material and erased on request; idle phones are forgotten automatically. See [PRIVACY.md](../security/PRIVACY.md).
- Vault text and images, and conversation text, now reach the owner's phone (Notes and History scopes). Before spec 028 vault content left the PC only toward a model provider ([vault-and-knowledge](vault-and-knowledge.md), [security](security.md)). Nothing goes to a new third party and nothing is stored on the phone; responses are `no-store`, the service worker never handles `/api/`, and the PWA CSP allows `data:` images only (camera stills and note images), never a remote one.
- A stolen, unlocked phone with History and «continue conversations» on can make an agent work inside a desktop conversation. The approval policy is the app's normal one: in «Permitir todo» mode `exec` and `write` do not ask, a phone-started run included; `publish` always asks. Narrowing it is not done in this spec ([R‑14](../security/RISKS.md)).
- The remote chat agent can still call `read_note` and `search_knowledge` in the shared remote thread; the Notes scope gates the direct reader only.
- **Files from the phone (spec 031).** The PC re-checks every file on the bytes with spec 030's rules (the phone's checks are a convenience), refuses a secret-looking name with no override and strips picture metadata; the provider notice is acknowledged on the PC only (the dialog that turns the switch on, or the desktop notice) and covers the conversation's provider and the vision provider, not sub-agent providers. A stolen, unlocked phone with Chat and Send files on can park up to 5 files of 700 000 bytes for 15 minutes each and send them for the agent to read ([R‑14](../security/RISKS.md) (s)); there is no step-up and no approval card for a file because it is not a tool. A put restarts the conversation's 30-day clock even if the file is never sent. Audit entries carry kind and size, never a name or id. The protected security layer was edited for this spec and its manifest hashes were recomputed (T44); any later edit of a protected file must recompute them.
- **Alma's company tools on the phone (spec 029).** `create_company` is offered in the phone's shared remote thread and, as on the desktop, in a conversation continued by id unless that conversation is a voice or WhatsApp one, and its approval is answered under the approve scope like any other, once, never «Siempre». `ApprovalView` gained `whole`, set by `canAnswerFrom('phone', approval, 1500)`: when the whole input does not fit, `ApprovalsFlow.answer` returns `already-answered` to an allow (the approval stays pending for the desktop and Telegram), the PWA hides Permitir and shows «see it on the PC» (`approvalActions`, `remote.pwa.approve.seeOnPc`), and a deny still goes through. An allow that gets past the flow carries the channel `phone` into the broker, which checks the same rule again and records the channel in the audit entry `company.create`. The phone shows the tool's raw input cut at 1500 characters, not the desktop card. Pending: the walk on a real phone has not been done.
- Audit entries for notes and conversations carry the device, the outcome and a vault-relative path or a conversation id, never text or titles.

## Known limits

- Needs Tailscale on PC and phone with the same account; without it the channel reports `tailscaleDown` and the wizard explains how to install it. Serve setup and the human Tailscale tasks (S2) are tracked separately from this spec's code.
- The phone needs the PC on and Alma running; there is no offline queue beyond the cached shell. The relay (spec 027) buffers nothing either: a phone request while the PC is off answers «offline».
- The relay is built and not deployed. Its limits that remain to verify on the real platform are listed under «Deploying»; its honest limit is that an operator who deliberately serves a hostile phone app can read one person's traffic (R-14 (n)).
- 031 limits: a file from the phone is at most 700 000 bytes (bigger files, and a picture that cannot be shrunk to fit or a GIF over the limit, are sent from the PC); no thumbnails, previews or downloads of attachments on the phone, only names; no chunked or resumable upload and nothing queued while the PC is off; a put into a conversation with a run in progress is refused (`busy`); pending uploads are not purged when only History or continue is turned off; a lost answer leaves an orphan pending upload until it expires; a send that loses its connection shows «not delivered» rather than «PC unreachable»; `StatusView.sendFiles` is optional on the wire.
- iOS may evict the browser storage (keys and cache) under pressure; then the phone must pair again.
- Renaming the PC (its Tailscale hostname) invalidates keys and passkeys: every phone must pair again. The machine name is visible in TLS certificate logs.
- «No internet» on the phone only reflects what the OS reports (PD-13); a captive portal can lie.
- 028 limits: notes are read-only (no create, edit, rename or delete from the phone); no content or semantic search from the phone; company conversations and company vaults are not reachable; only the active vault is served; the phone cannot start a conversation with a chosen agent or change a conversation's agent, model or folder; the 2-connections-per-device limit of 026 is still not enforced (026 AC-10.3); a rename of an excluded folder outside Alma leaves it listed as stale until the person fixes it; the real-phone checks (screen reader, 200 % text, landscape, keyboard over the compose box) are manual.
